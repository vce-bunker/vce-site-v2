Halo functional group
- F, Cl, Br or I
- polar chloro FG
- strongest IMF: dipole-dipole

Hydroxyl
- polar
- suffix: -ol
- prefix: hydroxy-
- more reactive than their corresponding alkane
- miscible in water (soluble) due to H bonds
- alkanol is same as alcohol
- Small alcohols are soluble in H2O because the polar Hydroxyl functional group can form hydrogen bonds with water
- strongest IMF: H bonds $\therefore$ alcohols have ^ BP/MP
- you need a bracket for semi structural formular around the OH as that by itself counts as a branch

Ketones/aldehydes
- polar carbonyl FG
- diple-dipole forces between 2 carbonyls (c to the o of the other molecule)

Carboxyl
- polar carboxyl FG
- H bonds:
	- need to draw the dipoles, and also use dipoles for explanations
	- always delta + on the C and H, and delta - on the O
	- use full name (hydrogen bonds not h bonds)
	- having a carboxyl dimer also increases molar mass (chain size) and this dispersion forces
		- because having a dimer is as strong as becoming one molecule

Amines
- H bonds between 2 amines (N...H-N)
	- delta + on the H, delta - on the N
- polar amino FG (not amine!)
