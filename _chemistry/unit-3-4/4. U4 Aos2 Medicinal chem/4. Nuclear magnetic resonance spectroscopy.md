(NMR spectroscopy)
- only technique that can deduce precise 3D structure
- uses radio waves (lower energy than infrared)
- only works with nuclei with odd mass number
	- if no odd mass number, does not have "spin"
- the nuclei have their own magnetic field (they are magnets)
- when an external magnetic field is applied, the nuclei either align with it or align against it
	- those that align with are lower energy
		- more common, more stable
- different nuclei have different thresholds of rf energy absorption that cause them to flip to antiparallel. This is recorded.
	- 1H-NMR (detect absorbance of frequencies that excite different H1 atoms)
	- 13C-NMR

Environment:
- attached in the same way (same neighbouring atoms)
- positioned equally relative to unpaired electrons

Nuclei with varying environments are shielded to varying degrees to the applied magnetic field and hence the energy required to flip is different

**Interpretation**
- The number of sets of peaks
	- indicates the number of different hydrogen environments
- chemical shift
	- the functional group to which the judrocarbons are attached is identified by the amount of chemical shift, as compared to the TMS standard
	- nuclei closer to electronegative elements (eg O) have higher ppm (more left)
- Peak splitting
	- the number of peaks that can be used to identify the number of neighbouring hydrogens found in a similar chemical environment. Using the n+1 rule, the number of hydrogens can be determined
	- there is one extra peak for each hydrogen attached to a neighbouring carbon to the carbon the H is attached to
- Peak area
	- the total area under the peaks indicates the number of equivalent hydrogens present (number of hydrogens in a given environment)
