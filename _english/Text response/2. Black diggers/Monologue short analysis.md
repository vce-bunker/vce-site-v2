What do the key monologues reveal about the experience of the war about aboriginal australians



**Ghost**
The purpose of the ghost speech is to remind the audience of the importance for aborigines to die in their home country. It aims to emphasize the sadness that IA feel if they are separated from their home country.

The ghost first recounts some happy memories of his childhood. He remembers "sitting there and laughing" and "stretch[ing] out [his] back".

Then he states how well he performed in the war, killing many soldiers, defeating "three machine-gun nests in one movement" and captured "maybe thirty, forty prisoners" he also got a medal (the DCM). Emphasizes the significant contribution he had, he deserves a good life.

But this does not happen as he then states he dies suddenly and "wasn't heading home". Instead he was forced to eternally wander on lands that "aren't his own"


**Bloke with wine glass**
Provides another perspective from a different experience, asserting that not all aboriginals shared the sad, regretful experience and actually were grateful for the war

The bloke begins by recalling some horrible experiences to demonstrate that even though he thought it was good the bad things could not be ignored, there is always terrible things about the war and that it should be avoided in the future.

He recalls waking up with "mud and human waste in [his] nose [,] eyes [,] your hair [and] mouth" eyes caked with "dried tears and pus from the infections", amongst other bad things.

However he then continues on  to state that it was worth it- "thank god for the chance to serve"

He mentions that serving in the war helped him gain a sense of belonging, and that he "found myself identifying with Australia"

He states that after the war "brought [him] into the fold" and gave him a sense of community and connections white whites that he otherwise wouldn't have if the war hadn't occurred. This is shown in his change in attitude, that he is now cheering for Dave Sands not because he is aboriginal but he is Australian.


**Old soldier (ern) p.91**

This monologue aims to reflect on the lasting damage of war.

The old soldier recalls his experiences after the war, stating that by his thirties he "barely gave his experience a second thought". However he then recalls how pieces of shell had started emerging from his skin, reminding him of the war.

He described it as "iron harvest", demonstrating that past events cannot be "buried" and "forgotten" and will always "inch its way [back] up", highlighting the lasting impacts of soldiers' experiences on their lives.

When he returns to meet his friends at the bar, they believed that "for a long time" he was "dead", emphasizing the length of time he felt emotionally scarred after the war.

The exiting of the shell pieces also reflects the legacy of the soldiers slowly working their way out of society.

**Speech** (fake)
English black diggers speech

Good morning retired servicemen and women,

As I'm sure you all know, today is ANZAC day. It's an important day for all of us, who spent years overseas, in horrific conditions, to fight for our country. I am sure your losses and experiences must have been unimaginably difficult, and is something that not many would be able to mentally endure. As a historian I have read a lot about your efforts and I must emphasize that Australia is deeply grateful for your service.

However, what I am here to speak to you today for, is not only to acknowledge your experiences and efforts, but to focus on a smaller, often overlooked group of people.

These people worked hard in the war, just like you. They experienced the pain, the hardships, that you all faced in the war.

But despite all this, after the war,

They had their pensions and income quarantined, 
Had decreased opportunity in employment,
They were denied access to soldier settlement schemes,
and even lost their homes to soldiers.

They were excluded from participation in ANZAC marches, military funerals, and access to (RSL) clubs.
They faced significant prejudice in civil rights and received little to no public or private support.

These people, are the Black Diggers.

Well "why put all that effort in?" you may ask.
"how will fighting in the war even benefit them?"

Equality, recognition, respect. Things that many of you take for granted.

But all of that was lost after the war. All their efforts, all their sacrifices, death of indigenous friends and permanent damage to physical and mental health. It was all to be called "mate". And that did not even last long.

So I ask today, on ANZAC day. I ask all of you. Please support them, please stand up for them. As they deserve it. As we all deserve it. To be recognised as part of the ANZACs. Thank you.
