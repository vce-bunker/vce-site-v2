The film 12 Angry men, written in 1957 during the McCarthyist era, in the midst of turmoil and paranoia, was a time in which justice and democracy were tenuously balanced on fear and hysteria, and innuendo was enough to condemn. In response, author reginald rose and director sidney lumet's courtroom drama expose the uncertain nature of the justice system and assert that for justice to be facilitated, one must overcome their apathy, bias and prejudice. Furthermore, they illusstrate that through using one's background experiences and knowledge to instead promote thoughtful deliberation rather than impulsive debate is a vital pillar of a healthy juror system and democracy. Overall Rose and lumet hope to encourage people to be skeptical of immediate opinions and beliefs and search for possible doubts in order to promote a fair and just society.
